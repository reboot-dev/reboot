# Access policy

An **access policy** decides who may reach your application at all.
It stands in front of every route and every RPC: a visitor who has
not been let in sees nothing of your application, not even its own
sign-in page.

That makes it different from [sign-in](/users/oauth), which says who
your application's _users_ are. Sign-in is part of your application;
the access policy is the door in front of it. The two compose: a
visitor the policy lets in then signs in to your application as
usual, and an MCP client goes through your application's own OAuth
flow exactly as it would without a policy.

The typical use is running your application on a public URL for your
own MCP clients, without letting the rest of the internet in.

## Let in only yourself

Sign in to the Reboot Cloud once:

```sh
rbt login
```

Then run your application with `--access=user`:

```sh
rbt dev run --access=user ...
```

Only you can reach it now, on every address it is served at, your
tunnel's URL and `localhost` alike. Point a browser at it and you are
sent through the Reboot Cloud to sign in, once per browser; after
that the browser carries a host-scoped `rbt_access` cookie, good for
twelve hours, and everything works as before. Anything that is not a
browser, an MCP client or a `curl`, gets a `401` until it holds a
credential your application minted, which it can only get by signing
in through a browser that was let in.

When you give `rbt dev run` a `--tunnel-url` and you are signed in,
`--access=user` is the default, and `rbt dev run` says so. Pass
`--access=public` to let in anyone.

:::note What the Cloud learns
Letting you in asks the Reboot Cloud for a token for your
application's origin (RFC 8707 `resource`). That token is good for
nothing but telling your application who signed in: it does not let
your application act as you on the Cloud, and the Cloud never sees
your application's traffic.
:::

## Sign out

Visit `/__/access/signout` on your application to forget that your
browser was let in; `rbt logout` forgets your sign-in to the Cloud.

## Under `rbt serve`

The policy is configured by two environment variables, which
`rbt dev run --access=user` sets for you:

| Variable | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `REBOOT_ACCESS_POLICY` | `user:<id>`: let in one user of the issuer, by their id there, and nobody else. |
| `REBOOT_ACCESS_ISSUER` | The origin of the OAuth authorization server that says who a visitor is, e.g. `https://cloud.prod1.rbt.cloud`. |

Set both for a deployment that only you should reach; leave both
unset for one that is open.

## Limits

- Python applications only. A TypeScript application refuses to start
  under a policy rather than run open.
- The policy stands in front of what your application serves. Under
  `rbt dev run --frontend-url`, your frontend's dev server answers
  `/__/frontend/**` itself, and that stays reachable.
